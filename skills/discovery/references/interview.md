# Discovery interview script

Seven rounds. Each has base questions, follow-ups depending on what you hear, and warning
signs. At most four questions per turn; adapt the order to the conversation. After each round,
summarize with "I understand that..." and wait for confirmation.

## Round 1 · The idea in one sentence

Base:
1. Tell me the idea the way you would tell a friend, in one sentence.
2. What problem does it solve and what happens today without it?
3. Is it a new project or does something already exist (site, app, Instagram account, spreadsheet)?
4. Why now?

Follow-ups:
- If the sentence has more than one idea: "Which of the two is the main one if you could only do one?"
- If it describes a solution ("an app with..."): "What would the person do with that? Walk me through a normal day."

Warning: the answer talks about technology and not about people.

## Round 2 · Success and failure

Base:
1. In twelve months, how will we know it worked? What number moves?
2. What is that number today, if it exists?
3. What would make you consider the project a failure even if the site is pretty and works?
4. Is there a date or event that marks the launch?

Follow-ups:
- If they say "more clients": "How many more, and how do we count them: form, call, sale?"
- If there is no metric: propose one and ask whether it makes sense.

Warning: success is defined by what the site does, not by what the business achieves.

## Round 3 · Users and their tasks

Base:
1. Who will use it? Describe two concrete people to me, with a name if you like.
2. What does each one do in the first five minutes inside?
3. What do they use today instead and what bothers them about it?
4. Can we talk to two or three of them this week?

Follow-ups:
- If there are more than three profiles: "Which one pays or decides? Let's start there."
- If there are users with accounts: "What does a signed-in person see that a visitor does not?"

Warning: "everyone" as the audience. Ask for the first real customer.

## Round 4 · Context: what exists and competitors

Base:
1. What exists already: domain, brand, logo, colors, copy, photos, site, social, analytics?
2. Who are the direct competitors? Give me two or three sites.
3. Which sites do you admire, even outside the sector, and what about them?
4. Which sites do you dislike and why?

Follow-ups:
- If there is a current site: "What works and what does not? Do you have access to the analytics?"
- If there is a brand: "Is there a manual or just the logo?"

Warning: the current site has traffic and nobody mentioned keeping the URLs.

## Round 5 · Data, money and access

Base:
1. What information about people is stored? Name, email, phone, address, payments, health, minors.
2. Are there payments? One-off, subscription, in person?
3. Are there users who sign in? Different types or permissions?
4. Do visitors upload anything: files, comments, reviews, profiles?

Follow-ups:
- If there is personal data: "Where do those people live? Mexico, United States, Europe?" and "How long must it be kept?"
- If there are payments: "Do you already have an account with Stripe, Mercado Pago, Conekta or another?"
- If there is login: "What happens if someone gets into someone else's account?" to size the impact.

Warning: sensitive data mentioned in passing. This feeds Schneier directly.

## Round 6 · Constraints and people

Base:
1. What is the deadline and what sets it?
2. Is there a budget for tools, hosting, domain, photos, copy?
3. Who approves each step and who produces the copy and images?
4. Languages? Accessibility required by contract or sector?

Follow-ups:
- If more than one person approves: "Who breaks the tie?"
- If the user produces the content: "When will you have it? It is what delays projects the most."

Warning: fixed deadline with no content ready and no content owner.

## Round 7 · Scope and close

Base:
1. What is explicitly out of this first version?
2. What is non-negotiable?
3. What worries you might go wrong?
4. Is there anything I have not asked that I should know?

Follow-ups:
- If "everything is a priority": "If the deadline were cut in half, what stays?"

Close with the full summary of the seven rounds and ask for confirmation before researching.

## Users · five questions for interviews with real users

For the user to ask, or for you if they connect you with them. Twenty minutes.

1. Tell me about the last time you tried to [the main task]. What did you do step by step?
2. What was the hardest or most annoying part?
3. What did you use? Why that and not something else?
4. If it disappeared tomorrow, what would you do?
5. What would something new need to have for you to try it?

Never ask "would you use X?" or "would you like it if...?". People say yes to be polite. Ask
about what they did, not what they would do.
